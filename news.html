v0.20.72 Patch

The Benches [Feature]

Crafting is a bench you put things on. Four slots in the middle of the window: drag items out of your bag into them and the bench works out what you are making. Order never matters. Right-click a slot to take an item back, drag another in to replace it. Pick a recipe from the list instead and it loads your own copies into the slots for you.

You see exactly which copy is going in, tier frame and colours and all, and that copy is the one consumed. Under each slot is what you put in over what the craft takes: 5,000/100. Drop a whole stack and it all goes in, so one drop can cover fifty crafts.

Break Down: gear you have outgrown is raw material. Hand a piece over with a Zeni fee and it comes apart into infusion materials. Better gear gives better materials, and strong pieces carry a chance of something from the tier above. It never fails. Infused and recoloured pieces break down too, for exactly what a plain one gives.

Luck earns its keep. It improves a chance by a share of itself, so 10% Luck turns a 10% chance into 11%, and it buys extra rolls on any range and keeps the best. It applies to what a break-down yields, whether a bonus lands, the value an infusion rolls, which drop table is picked, and how many of a thing that table drops.

A table only shows its own work, and anything you are missing ingredients for is greyed out, icon and all. A recipe you cannot afford still shows what it wants instead of going blank.

The infusion bench matches. The piece on the bench sits in a slot of its own, so you always know which copy you are working on. Every infusable piece you own is listed, greyed until the next tier's materials are in your bag.

The guide has a new Luck & modifiers page, and the crafting and infusion pages cover all of the above.

Break It Down [Feature]

v0.20.49 Patch

Mending [Feature]

Three healing techniques, and a Namekian discipline behind them.

  • Recovery Wave. Hands to the sky and your own ki turned inward. Closes a wound at once, then keeps knitting for eight seconds.
  • Shared Vitality. The same ki sent across the gap into a chosen ally. Lands harder than healing yourself does, and works on them for ten seconds after. It has nothing to give an enemy.
  • Sanctuary Bloom. Ki spills out of you and settles over the ground as a ring of green light. Everyone standing with you is mended where they stand and keeps mending for twelve seconds.
  • Grace of Namek, a passive. Every positive effect you place lands 35% stronger and holds 50% longer, and your own body knits quietly on its own.

A heal only ever reaches your side. An area heal finds you, your party, and your team in a match — and nothing else. Not the thing you are fighting, and not the player fighting you in a PvP zone, who cannot be topped up by accident or on purpose. Aiming Shared Vitality at an enemy is simply a miscast.

Everything Since The Last Post

Combat feel

  • Aim while you cast: line spells and projectiles steer through the whole wind-up, sprite and telegraph following. Charges aim where you point at release instead of inheriting the last charge's direction.

v0.20.44 Patch

You Lead, The Server Follows

Your character faces where you point, full stop. Facing is now yours: you turn the instant you press, and the server takes your word for it instead of working out its own answer from movement samples and overwriting you a round trip later. Quick direction changes stick without holding the key down.

A tap that starts and ends between two frames still turns you. That is the fastest press a keyboard can report, and it used to register as nothing at all.

Being slowed no longer drags you backwards. v0.20.43 Patch

Facing, Trails And Effect Icons

Your character faces where you pressed, even on a bad connection. You would tap a direction, turn, and then get turned back for a moment: an update computed before the server saw the key was overwriting your own turn. Updates that predate the key are now ignored until the server catches up.

Re-aiming a charge turns you. The body, the held pose and the ball in your hand all follow the new direction while you steer, instead of staying pointed where the charge started and only snapping at release.

Sprint after-images follow one rule: under you, unless you are seen from behind. It reads the sprite that is actually on screen, so nothing puts a trail across your chest while you are plainly facing the camera.

Active effects are just their icons. No frames, no boxes, drawn at their own size. An effect with no art of its own borrows the icon of the technique or the gear that granted it.

v0.20.42 Patch

Taps, Trails And The Character Sheet

A tapped direction now counts. Your input is sampled thirty times a second, so a key pressed and released between two samples never left your machine: you turned on screen and the server never heard about it, and the shot went the old way. It felt like lag and it was not. A tap is now held until the next sample, so short taps turn you for real.

Charged attacks steer again. Re-aiming during a charge was reading the movement input, which a cast switches off, so the server got nothing at all and the shot left in whatever direction the charge opened with. Charges follow your aim to the moment of release now, which is what made this feel worst on them.

Sprint after-images pick their side by where they are, not by which way you happen to be facing. Running east while facing north put every echo on top of you; that is the flicker. They also no longer spend their first frame arriving from somewhere else.

Character sheet:

  • The scrollbar no longer sits on top of the stat XP bars.
  • Speed and the three regen rates read as two columns in the same type as the stats above them, instead of four near-empty lines.
  • Active effects are icons in a grid, grouped by where they came from. Hover one for the full breakdown: what it does, how long is left, and where it came from.
v0.20.41 Patch

Solum Sorts Properly

On Solum and Muscle Tower you draw where you stand again. v0.20.37 Patch

Coins, Six Deep

Zeni drops in six coins now. Small copper is 1, big copper 10, small silver 100, small gold 1,000, big silver 10,000, big gold 100,000. so a purse of any size lands as a handful you can read at a glance instead of a heap.

Locus Warp Netcode Fix

Big netcode fix for everyone who has been fighting rubberbanding after stepping through a warp. Walking between loci (dimensions), houses and other scenes should now feel exactly as smooth as the overworld, even on a rough connection.

  • After any warp your freshly loaded character predicted movement with default stats and a full stamina bar, while the server kept your real, often drained, stamina. v0.16.5 Patch

Sprint Smoothing

Sprinting on a high-ping connection is smooth now. The snap-back is gone.

Why it's so much better: when you move, your game predicts where you're going *instantly* while the server confirms it a moment later. The two stay in sync by remembering your recent moves and comparing notes. The bug: that memory was too short for laggy connections, and worse, it was throwing away the exact moves it needed to compare against, so the game kept guessing wrong and yanking you back by hundreds of pixels. We made that memory longer, stopped it from discarding what it still needs, and pointed it at the right moment to compare. Now your game and the server agree on where you are to within a few pixels instead of a few hundred, and the tiny leftover difference blends in invisibly instead of snapping.

Tested at half a second of lag: a 166-pixel rubberband is now about 10 pixels.
